Safety and AlertsUpdated 24 Nov 2026

Safety and Alerts¶

SMART raises an alert when someone enters a zone they should stay out of, presses the SOS button on their device, or when a tracking device is unplugged from a machine. It tells the right people straight away by email and SMS, and keeps a record of each alert until someone closes it.

How alerts work¶

Three things decide who hears about an alert:

  • Contacts: the people who receive alerts. Each contact has a name and email address, and optionally a phone number for SMS alerts. A contact doesn’t need a SMART login.

  • Alert rules: what to alert on, when, and who to tell. A rule lists one or more events (Exclusion breach, SOS, Unplug), the days and times it is active, and its contacts.

  • Zones: where a rule applies. You assign each rule to the exclusion zones, alert zones or workzones it covers.

When a breach or SOS happens inside a zone, SMART contacts everyone on the active rules assigned to that zone:

Contact

Receives

First responder on the rule

SMS and email

Other contacts on the rule

Email

Rules with the Unplug event email every contact on the rule.

If no active rule has recipients for an SOS, SMART still sends it to all of the project’s contacts.

Alert Management¶

Select Alerts in the navigation panel, then Alert Management. The screen has three panels side by side: Contacts, Alert Rules and the zones. Selecting a contact, rule or zone highlights everything linked to it in the other two panels.

Screenshot

Alert Management with the Contacts, Alert Rules and zones panels

Contacts¶

  • Add a contact: select Create Contact, enter their name, email and phone number, then select Create.

  • Edit or delete: use the pencil or bin button on the contact’s row. Deleting a contact removes them from every rule.

  • Add a contact to a rule: select the rule in Alert Rules, then select Assign to rule (the plus button) on the contact’s row. Remove from rule takes them off again.

  • Make someone a first responder: with the rule selected, select Set as First Responder on their row. A contact can be a first responder on one rule and a regular recipient on another.

The filter above the list shows All Contacts, Assigned Only, Unassigned Only or First Responders.

Alert rules¶

Select Create Rule to open Create Alert Rule.

  1. Enter a Rule Name that says what the rule is for, such as “After hours security”.

  2. Choose the Events it covers: Exclusion breach, SOS and Unplug.

  3. Leave Enabled on. Disabled rules don’t send alerts.

  4. Optionally set Active From and Active Until dates. Leave them empty for a rule that starts now and runs indefinitely.

  5. Under Active Schedule, add a time window for each period the rule should alert. Each window has a start day and time and a finish day and time, in the project’s time zone and in 24-hour time. For an overnight shift, finish on the next day (for example Monday 22:00 to Tuesday 02:00). The shortcut buttons fill in common schedules, such as Business Hours (Monday to Friday, 9 am to 5 pm) or 24/7.

  6. For exclusion breaches, choose any Allowed Resource Roles (Excluded from Alerts). People in these roles can enter the zone during the rule’s hours without raising an alert. The exemption covers only the roles you select, so select child roles as well if they also need it.

  7. Check the Rule Preview, then select Create Rule.

A rule with no time windows never alerts. If a zone has several rules, each one applies separately: a role allowed by one rule still raises alerts under another.

The Create Alert Rule window

Assigning rules to zones¶

  1. Select the rule in Alert Rules.

  2. In the zones panel, choose Exclusion Zones, Alert Zones or Work Zones.

  3. Select Assign (the plus button) on each zone the rule should cover. The minus button removes it.

Exclusion zones are grouped by zone type. To assign the selected rule to every zone of a type in one go, use Assign rule to all on the type’s heading (or Unassign rule from all to remove it). This only affects the zones in the group today. Zones you add later need the rule assigned separately.

Rules don’t pass down from a parent zone to its child zones. Assign the rule to every zone that needs it.

Checking who will be alerted¶

The coloured dot beside each zone shows its alert set-up, across all of its rules:

Colour

Meaning

Grey

No rule assigned

Yellow

Rules assigned, but they have no contacts

Orange

At least one contact will be emailed

Red

At least one first responder will be contacted

The colour stays the same outside a rule’s schedule. The Alert Actions column shows how each zone will alert (first responder SMS, first responder email, contact email). Hover over an icon to see the names.

To test a set-up, open Zones from the navigation panel and select the lightning button above a table: Simulate exclusion breach on Exclusion zones, or Simulate alert on Alert Zones for an SOS. Choose a resource and a zone, and SMART previews who would be emailed or sent an SMS. The simulation sends nothing unless you turn on Send actual alerts.

Alert workflows¶

Every alert opens a workflow: a record of the alert, who was told and what was done about it.

When an SOS comes in¶

  • A red banner appears at the top of the map: Active SOS Alert (1) - Device carrier not contacted. It turns yellow once the status changes to contacted. Select the banner to open the workflows.

  • New alerts also appear in the alert feed on the map. Select an alert to go to it on the map, or select the cross to dismiss it from the feed.

The SOS banner and the alert feed on the map

The Alert Workflows table¶

Select Alerts in the navigation panel, then Alert Workflows. Workflows for the selected time period are grouped first into Live and Simulations, then by status, then by alert type.

Status

Meaning

Active - Not Contacted

Nobody has reached the person yet

Active - Contacted

Someone is dealing with it

Resolved

Closed

Each row shows the resource, the details (zone, location, category, level), the notifications sent, comments and status. Hover over a notification icon to see who was sent what. Show Location takes you to the alert on the map.

Screenshot

The Alert Workflows table

Updating and closing a workflow¶

  1. Select Edit Workflow (the pencil) on the row.

  2. Change the Status, choose a Category (for example False Alarm or Medical Emergency) and add an External Reference if your incident system has one.

  3. Add comments as you go: select Add Comment, type up to 1,000 characters, then save the comment.

  4. To close the alert, set Status to Resolved and describe the Cause. SMART records who closed it and when.

  5. Select Save.

A resolved workflow is locked and can’t be edited.

Breach and SOS history¶

SMART keeps every breach and SOS, whether or not anyone was emailed about it.

  • Breach History in Analytics shows exclusion zone breaches over time, by zone, type and activity.

  • The Alert Zones table lists each zone’s SOS alerts. Select Go to SOS to see one on the map.

  • On the map, turn on Resolved SOS in Map layers to show closed SOS alerts as well as active ones.
